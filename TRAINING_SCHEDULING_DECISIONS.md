# Training and scheduling decisions

Accepted context updated **19 October 2026**. Research searched **18 October 2026**. This is the rationale brief; [PRODUCT_SPEC.md §8](PRODUCT_SPEC.md#8-flexible-training-agenda) owns the behavior contract and [REVIEW_VERIFICATION.md](REVIEW_VERIFICATION.md) owns implementation evidence. Preferences and reasonable inferences below are not physiological facts.

## Accepted user context and boundaries

- Muscle growth comes first. Target: **four gym sessions/week**. Reported long-term average: **3.5/week**, also acceptable. Four sessions average a seven-day cycle at the target, or eight days at the historical average. Neither implies a Monday reset or a catch-up quota.
- Preserve the rolling **Push A → Pull A → Push B → Pull B** and current exercises. Leg work occurs in every session. No automatic substitutions, B-session swaps, recovery scores or mandatory recovery intervals.
- Current Norwegian 4×4 frequency is roughly once per two weeks; desired frequency is once per **7–10 days**, subordinate to gym scheduling. No planned gym workout on that day. Runs can normally be in the evening. Saturday evening lifting followed by Sunday evening running is about 24 hours start-to-start, not the previously assumed 16 hours; neither guarantees recovery.
- Reported VO₂max is approximately 51, from a Garmin Fēnix 8 using a Garmin Pro chest strap. The user expects improvement potential but does not want to prioritize it over lifting. This is a wearable estimate, not a laboratory measurement.
- The user means **five heart-rate zones**. One short easy run may occur; its scheduling is outside the first implementation. Walking, hiking and cycling can occur incidentally or as explicit calendar activities.
- Office days usually mean no training on Monday and Thursday, but can change, including Tuesday and Friday. Allow individual dates and recurring-pattern changes at short notice. Explicit rest is a user choice, not automatically inferred from a hike or biometric score.
- Adding an activity that happened today or a few days ago should record it without an exercise checklist. A future appointment is still a plan. After Garmin integration, imported activities are the default actual record, with a labelled manual override that survives sync and can be reverted.
- Build the manual scheduler first; defer new Garmin integration until its planning/recording semantics are tested. Preserve the existing bridge. No publication is authorized by the implementation request.

## Inputs actually inspected

The source of programming truth is `src/data/core_protocol.json` v1.1.0. The engine's 50% automatic completion, same-day edits, manual Finish, cycle rollover, Undo, stable session IDs and IndexedDB transaction behavior were inspected. Completion records are adherence indicators, not proof that every prescribed set was performed.

No usable longitudinal personal training export was available. Two sparse bridge queue rows were not treated as a training history. The 3.5/week average, VO₂max estimate, run frequency and preferences are user reports. Actual running duration, pace, time in zones, terrain, soreness and repeated lift performance remain unknown; the implementation must not invent them.

| Session | Actual protocol, working sets |
|---|---|
| Push A, 15 sets | Low-incline DB press 3; reverse hack squat 3; cable fly 2; machine lateral raise 3; leg extension 2; overhead cable triceps extension 2 |
| Pull A, 15 sets | Barbell RDL 3; neutral pull-up 3; seated leg curl 2; chest-supported row 3; reverse pec deck 2; incline DB curl 2 |
| Push B, 15 sets | High-incline Smith press 3; leg extension 3; seated DB overhead press 2; cable fly 2; machine lateral raise 3; triceps pushdown 2 |
| Pull B, 16 sets | Lat pulldown 3; seated leg curl 3; wide cable row 3; standing calf raise 3; face pull 2; preacher curl 2 |

Compounds generally target 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR); most isolations 0–1, lateral raises 0–2. Failure is optional for a stable final isolation set. Controlled eccentric and full pain-free ROM do not prescribe a fixed tempo. Across 61 sets/cycle: quads 8, hamstrings 8, calves 3, chest-oriented work 10, main back work 12, direct biceps/triceps 4 each, lateral raises 6. Supporting muscle work is not converted into invented fractional sets. RDL and hack squat both involve glutes; pressing involves triceps/anterior deltoids; rows and pulls involve elbow flexors. Alternating names do not mean zero overlap.

At an eight-day cycle, eight quad or hamstring sets correspond to seven sets/week. Extending a seven-day cycle to ten days lowers the same prescribed volume per week by 30%. Preserving tolerable gym attendance is therefore relevant to hypertrophy; more calendar spacing is not automatically better.

## Training recommendation and strongest alternative

**Default: retain alternation, introduce the desired low-frequency 4×4 conservatively, and judge it by gym performance and running tolerance.** This is a reasonable trial compatible with the user's priorities, not a proven optimal dose or a promise of a large VO₂max gain. One session every 7–10 days is less frequent than many successful interval studies, and less frequent than weekly training when the gap is ten days. Do not increase hard-running frequency just to reproduce a study if it displaces gym work.

**Strongest training alternative: selectively swap the B sessions** when actual hamstring/calf fatigue before a run matters more than quadriceps/pressing spacing. It changes which tissues are exposed near the run; it does not remove fatigue. It requires assessing the following cycle too. It is not an automatic scheduling rule in this implementation.

| Arrangement | Argument for | Argument against |
|---|---|---|
| Preserve Push/Pull alternation | More even distribution of direct pushing and pulling work; predictable rolling order; avoids grouping Pull A and Pull B simply to protect a run | Pull B's near-failure curls/calves may be close to running; glute and systemic overlap remains between alternating sessions |
| Selectively put Pull B before Push B | May increase elapsed time between hamstring/calf work and intervals; avoids RDL-style loading in the last gym session | Brings quad extensions closer to running; can compress Pull A → Pull B, then leave a long gap before the next Pull A; can compress Push B → next Push A under changed availability |

Illustrative three-cycle calculation, all sessions starting at 18:00, office Monday/Thursday: gym dates 3/4/6/7, 10/11/13/14, 17/18/20/21 November 2026; Sunday runs on 8/15/22 for this spacing comparison only. Preserved push-to-push and pull-to-pull gaps are 72, 96, 72, 96, 72 hours. Swapping only cycle 2's B sessions changes push gaps to 72, 96, 96, 72, 72 hours and pull gaps to 72, 96, 48, 120, 72 hours. Hamstrings/calves before the middle run move from about 24 to 48 hours, while quads move from 48 to 24. The 48-hour Pull A → Pull B interval includes RDL 3 + curl 2 sets, then curl 3 sets, as well as repeated back/biceps work. In another calendar, consecutive gym sessions might be separated by several days; consecutive calendar days need not contain gym sessions. Recompute elapsed time from actual dates/times, including the next cycle.

Acute soreness/fatigue, performance in the next session and long-term hypertrophy are different outcomes. An acute decrement is a reason to inspect dose/placement, not proof of reduced growth. Lack of soreness is not proof of recovery. Do not label any ordering optimal or enforce a universal 24/48/72-hour rule.

## Evidence supporting and limiting the decision

Original methods/results were examined for the decisive claims below. Studies were selected for dose, population and relevance, not recency alone. Findings are not individual forecasts.

| Original source | Population, dose and comparator | Findings, limitations and application |
|---|---|---|
| [Helgerud et al., 2007](https://rcc.hslu.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/downloads/sport/Aerobic_High-Intensity_Intervals_Improve_J.Helgerud_2007.pdf) | 40 moderately trained men, four groups, eight weeks, three sessions/week; oxygen-cost-matched continuous low/moderate work, 15/15 intervals and 4×4 at 90–95% HRmax with three-minute active recovery | Table 1: relative VO₂max in 4×4 group 55.5→60.4 ml/kg/min; the reported 7.2% refers to absolute oxygen uptake. Interval conditions outperformed continuous conditions, with no clear advantage between interval formats. Supports the method at this much larger dose, not once per 7–10 days alongside hypertrophy training. |
| [Lenk et al., 2025](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.14814/phy2.70573) | 26 completers of 44, 14 women, mean age 23; six weeks randomized to 1/2/3 weekly 4×4 sessions, progressing intensity; baseline lab VO₂max approximately 51 | Once-weekly table: 53.0→53.6, p=.60; twice 53.5→57.6, p=.02; thrice 48.5→51.2, p=.01. Frequency×time interaction p=.167. Once-weekly time-to-exhaustion also did not clearly improve. Small groups, high attrition, no inactive control; cannot infer statistically established superiority of higher frequency from within-group p-values, nor guaranteed once-weekly benefit. |
| [Zhang et al., 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1855612/full) | 45 active male university participants, 41 completers; six weeks 1/2/3 weekly 4×4 sessions; four-minute walking recoveries, unlike the classic three-minute format | VO₂max means 49.78→50.46, 49.04→53.58, 47.90→53.09 respectively; interaction p=.230. No inactive control, retrospective registration and four intolerance-related dropouts. Reinforces uncertainty about a small weekly dose; not proof of an optimal frequency and not a lifting-priority trial. |
| [Schumann et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/) | 43 controlled concurrent-training studies, 1,090 healthy adults; matched strength training with versus without endurance work; hypertrophy subset 15 studies/389 participants | Whole-muscle hypertrophy SMD −.01 (95% CI −.16,.18), maximal strength −.06 (−.20,.09); explosive strength −.28 (−.48,−.08). Same-session explosive-strength findings do not establish a universal separation cutoff. Mixed populations and modest study quality; supports compatibility on average, not zero interference for this runner. |
| [Lundberg et al., 2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40279-022-01688-x.pdf) | 15 studies, 300 participants; muscle-fiber hypertrophy, strength-only versus concurrent; 12 cycling studies, three running | Small negative fiber-level estimate, SMD −.23 (−.46,−.00), p=.050, substantial heterogeneity. Running/type-I subgroup rests on only three studies. Biopsy variability and limited samples caution against both alarmism and a claim that concurrent training cannot affect growth. |
| [Consecutive versus nonconsecutive resistance training, 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.00725/full) | 30 recreational men; 12 weeks, three weekly sessions; 24-hour versus 48–72-hour spacing, five exercises at 3×10 with 10RM loads | Strength and DXA lean mass improved without a clear group interaction. DXA is not local muscle-size imaging, participants were not advanced bodybuilders and this is not the present four-session protocol. Counters a universal mandatory recovery interval; does not prove all consecutive arrangements equivalent. |
| [Morán-Navarro et al., 2017](https://www.paulogentil.com/pdf/Time%20course%20of%20recovery%20following%20resistance%20training%20leading%20or%20not%20to%20failure.pdf) | Ten resistance-trained men; bench/squat 3×5, 6×5 or 3×10 with 10RM load; failure and nonfailure protocols including matched total repetitions; acute measures through 72 hours | Failure produced greater acute fatigue and longer recovery on measured outcomes. Nonfailure conditions left approximately five repetitions available, so this does not quantify 1 versus 2 RIR or establish a hypertrophy outcome. Supports monitoring actual effort and performance rather than deriving recovery from a workout name. |

The chest strap measures heart rate; the watch estimates VO₂max using its algorithm and activity inputs. Source therefore matters for the confidence placed in **51**, although it does not change the user's priority. [Garmin's Fēnix documentation](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-EECCAC99-90D6-4AB1-9A3A-EC433D3365E2/EN-GB/GUID-015C6746-435D-48E0-8ED8-CA2C81684E38.html) distinguishes the estimate and HR requirement. [Engel et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-025-05923-x) compared a Forerunner 245 with laboratory testing in 35 athletes; error varied with fitness. That is not Fēnix 8 validation and is not a correction factor for this user's number. Track consistent-device trends plus performance; a single estimate cannot establish a ceiling or quantify “huge potential.” A laboratory test would sharpen the estimate but is not required to start this modest scheduling trial.

## Zones, 80/20 and incidental activity

Three-zone research usually defines zone 1 below the first ventilatory/lactate threshold, zone 2 between the first and second thresholds, and zone 3 above the second. Five-zone watch systems divide intensity more finely and may use %HRmax, heart-rate reserve or threshold heart rate. Five-zone Z2 is not the same label as three-zone Z2. Exact mapping requires the user's zone method, boundaries, sport-specific settings and threshold quality.

Session-based classification counts the purpose/intensity of each session; time-based classification counts minutes in zones. One hard session and one easy session is 50% hard sessions, but not 50% hard minutes. A hypothetical 40-minute interval outing with 16 prescribed work minutes plus a 25-minute easy run gives 16/65 work minutes, about 25%; HR lag, recoveries and threshold definitions mean even that is not a measured physiological-zone ratio. This example must not become a manufactured 80/20 justification.

- [Seiler and Kjerland, 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00418.x): 11 elite junior skiers, mean VO₂max about 73, observed for 32 days. Of 384 sessions, 37 strength sessions were excluded. Session-HR classification was approximately 75/8/17%, but time-HR classification approximately 91/6.4/2.6%. This describes high-volume endurance practice and demonstrates how counting methods differ; it is not a minimum low-intensity quota for a hypertrophy-first routine.
- [Festa et al., 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070/full): 38 analyzed experienced recreational runners, eight weeks, polarized 77/3/20 versus focused 40/50/10 distributions with matched TRIMP but unequal time (29.9 versus 24.8 total hours). Both improved 2-km performance, without a clear between-group advantage or VO₂max improvement. Approximately 3–4 running hours/week and no matched hypertrophy program limit applicability here.
- [Rosenblat et al., 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02149-3): abstract-level background only; full text was not available for this review. The meta-analysis reports no overall polarized-versus-pyramidal advantage. It is not used as decisive methods-verified evidence.

Thus 80/20 is not a requirement for this low-volume plan. A reasonable trial can be justified by feasibility, tolerable dose and priorities without claiming it is polarized or optimal.

| Activity | Can contribute low-intensity endurance volume? | Information needed / limits |
|---|---|---|
| Short easy run | Yes, if sufficiently easy relative to the individual's thresholds | Duration, zone definitions, pace/HR, talk effort, hills and symptoms. It also adds running impacts. |
| Walking | Sustained brisk walking can; casual steps are useful activity but not automatically equivalent endurance training | Continuous duration, effort/HR relative to fitness, gradient. Much walking may be five-zone Z1; do not inflate Z2 totals. |
| Hiking | Some portions can; steep climbs can be moderate/hard | Duration, ascent/descent, pack, terrain, effort and muscle soreness. Descents can create substantial eccentric fatigue despite modest HR. |
| Cycling | Yes, if sustained at a genuinely low relative intensity | Duration, effort, sport-specific HR/power thresholds. Less running impact does not mean identical local fatigue or running adaptation. |
| Lifting | Not counted as low-intensity endurance minutes just because average HR lies in a zone | Intermittent contractions, rests and HR responses differ. It remains the priority strength/hypertrophy exposure. |

Aerobic fitness does not establish tolerance of running impact. Calf/Achilles, foot and other tissue tolerance depends on recent running exposure, terrain, footwear and progression. Do not infer that a fit cyclist or a VO₂max estimate of 51 tolerates a sudden increase in hard running. Keep the initial addition modest and review actual response.

## UX comparison and shared walkthroughs

Recommendation: **dated vertical agenda home**, with recent history close to Today and direct date movement. Strongest alternative: **Today-focused home with an accessible upcoming agenda**. Both must use exactly the same engine rules and full-change preview. The old 14-day drawer is not a retained requirement.

The agenda makes retrospective runs, changing office days and downstream effects easier to inspect directly. Its risk is pushing the immediate workout action out of view and adding scrolling. The Today alternative makes starting/resuming lifting prominent, but date edits and checking history require opening the agenda; hidden consequences must still be shown in the same preview. These are usability hypotheses, not measured superiority. The selected agenda retains a prominent Open/Resume workout action.

Shared illustrative baseline, November 2026, not the user's actual diary: recorded run 1; office Mon/Thu; gym cycle 1 PuA3/PlA4/PuB6/PlB7; planned run8; cycle2 PuA10/PlA11/PuB13/PlB14; cycle3 PuA17/PlA18/PuB20/PlB21. Dragging and tapping are equivalent intents, not different scheduling algorithms. An occupied fixed date must explain its blocker instead of silently swapping. Started/completed rows expose their fixed status before a gesture. [WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html) requires a nondrag single-pointer alternative; keyboard support alone is insufficient. A visible Move button with a date picker meets the intended interaction pattern, subject to actual accessibility testing.

Retrospective example: on16, moving the planned run18 to its actual date14 removes appointment18 and anchors the next 7–10-day preference to21–24. Adding a separate run14 keeps run18 and warns that it is only four days later. No completed gym history is rewritten; the future gym plan can legitimately remain unchanged.

## Validation and falsification

Training trial: compare a stable baseline with roughly 6–8 weeks of the added frequency, recognizing that this small dose may need longer to show aerobic change. Track completed gym sessions/cycle duration; load and reps at comparable RIR for RDL, hack squat/leg extension and curls/calves; recurring session-quality loss after runs/hikes; run duration/pace at comparable effort and conditions; impact-related discomfort and whether it changes the next workout. Record relevant sleep, illness, food/body-mass changes as context. A wearable trend alone does not prove an intervention effect; short-term strength changes do not directly measure hypertrophy.

Support for the recommendation: gym frequency remains acceptable, lift performance/progression does not show a persistent decline beyond normal variability, and running remains tolerable while performance or consistent-device aerobic trends improve. Repeated disruption to lifting or impact tolerance would argue for reducing/moving the run or testing a selective B swap. No improvement would falsify a claim that this dose reliably improves VO₂max; no such guarantee is made here.

Scheduling checks: reproduce all five scenarios; check stable identities across multiple cycles, earliest eligible forward placement, no lost/duplicated workouts, protected checks/sets/history, backdating add versus move, recurring exceptions, expired previews, transaction failure, concurrent windows, reload, export and whole-chain Undo. Date arithmetic must survive month and daylight-saving boundaries.

Usability tasks on both concepts: identify Today and start/resume the right gym session; enter a forgotten run; move onto an occupied date; change office days after a completed run; plan at least seven days ahead; recover from a cancelled gesture and Undo a saved cascade. Ask the user to predict the resulting dates before Apply. Observe wrong-date actions, unnoticed downstream changes, inability to discover Move, accidental drags while scrolling, time/taps to resume lifting and whether Undo's scope is understood. Prefer the alternative if the agenda repeatedly obstructs the lifting action without improving date-edit comprehension.

Implementation tests establish rule behavior, not real-world training benefit, physical-device gesture quality or Garmin reconciliation. The verification record must distinguish those evidence levels. Later Garmin work must preserve raw imported values, manual provenance/overrides, one-to-one activity matching and local logging during network failure; Body Battery and HRV provide context rather than invented muscle-specific recovery gates.

## Agenda review — 20 October 2026

Accepted UI revisions: preserve vertical dates and row geometry for past days, distinguish them with muted colors, label empty upcoming dates Rest, reduce the unused bottom inset and style the native scrollbar with existing theme colors. Rest copy does not create a fixed rest constraint. Selecting a date updates the summary and applicable workout action; Today restores the actual current date. History is the existing completed-gym archive with saved sets and export; manually recorded runs remain in the agenda.

Navigation proposal, not implemented or usability-validated: use three labelled bottom destinations, Home (house icon/agenda), Workout (resume the active workout) and Coach (existing assistant). Keep the four split-session selectors inside the workout surface and preserve its selected session when returning. This provides room for Home and exposes coaching from the agenda without six cramped dock controls. The trade-off is moving split-session switching away from its current bottom position; test switching and returning during a workout before adopting it. The current header Agenda action remains until that navigation decision is settled.

Follow-up accepted and implemented on 20 October: the user requested the missing bottom navigation, recorded weekly averages and phone credential entry for DeepSeek. These are design decisions, not a claim of measured usability superiority or a review by an external team.
